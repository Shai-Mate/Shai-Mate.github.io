Thanksgiving Dinner Timeline Printable (4-Week Countdown + Day-Of Plan)

Most Thanksgiving stress comes down to timing: the turkey, the sides and the pies all want the oven in the same two hours, and the shopping you forgot shows up on Thursday morning. A written timeline fixes both. Here's a 4-week countdown for US Thanksgiving on Thursday, November 26, 2026 (or any Friendsgiving), a simple way to plan the day itself, and a free one-page timeline to print.

The 4-week countdown

  • Four weeks out (late October): set the guest count and dinner time, and send the invitations. Ask every guest about allergies and diets now, not at the door.
  • Three weeks out: write the menu, then decide what you cook and what guests bring. If it's a potluck or Friendsgiving, hand out dish slots so you don't end up with four bags of rolls.
  • Two weeks out: order or buy the turkey, count chairs, plates and serving dishes, and borrow what's missing. Buy drinks and shelf-stable groceries.
  • One week out: clean out the fridge, plan the thawing schedule from your turkey's label or your recipe, and write the day-of timeline.
  • Two to three days out: fresh-food shopping, then make-ahead dishes: cranberry sauce, pie dough, chopped onions and celery, casseroles assembled but not baked.
  • The day before: bake pies, set the table, chill the drinks and put out serving dishes with a sticky note on each saying what goes in it.

How to plan the day: count back from dinner

Write your dinner time at the bottom of the page, then work upwards. The turkey sets the schedule, so place it first: take its total cook time and resting time from your recipe, and count back from dinner to find when it goes in. Next, add the sides that need the oven, then the stovetop jobs, then the things that only need reheating. Leave the last 30 minutes for gravy, carving and getting everything onto the table, because that half hour always takes longer than you think.

Put a name in the "Who" column for every row you're not doing yourself. Guests who offer to help are much more useful with a job ("mash the potatoes at 4:15") than with "let me know if you need anything".

Sharing one oven

  • Use the resting time. While the turkey rests under foil, the oven is free for the sides that bake at a similar temperature.
  • Group by temperature. List every oven dish with its temperature, and bake the ones that match together.
  • Move dishes off the oven. A slow cooker keeps mashed potatoes or stuffing warm, the stovetop handles gravy and vegetables, and many casseroles can be made the day before and simply reheated.
  • Ask potluck guests to bring dishes ready to serve, and mark on the sign-up sheet which dishes need oven time when they arrive.

An oven schedule is a planning tool, not cooking instructions: always use the times, temperatures and food-safety guidance in your own recipe and on the turkey's label.

After dinner

Have containers and labels ready before you sit down, so leftovers go into the fridge promptly and guests can take a plate home. Jot down what ran out and what nobody touched; next year's menu gets easier with that note. Black Friday is November 27 and Cyber Monday November 30, so a shopping list with a budget, written the night before, keeps the deals from turning into impulse buys.
