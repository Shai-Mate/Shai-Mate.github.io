Adapting it for kids, teens and adults

  • Ages 3-5: use a picture list instead of words and keep it to one or two rooms. Six to eight finds is plenty.
  • Ages 6-9: rhyming clues like the ones above, read aloud if needed. Give each child their own colour of card so siblings don't race for the same one.
  • Tweens and teens: make clues harder (riddles, a word scramble, a number code) and add a timer.
  • Adults: run it as a party game with points: 1 point for easy finds, 3 for hard ones, and a bonus for a photo with the item.

Christmas lights scavenger hunt: a simple plan

  1. Go after dark on a 20-30 minute drive or walk past streets known for displays.
  2. Give each spotter a list and a pencil on a clipboard; the driver only drives.
  3. Score as you go, then vote for best house and funniest display on the way home.
  4. Small prizes work best: a candy cane, or choosing the evening film. If the weather turns, switch to an indoor hunt.
