Cookie Exchange Rules Printable (Sign-Up Sheet + How Many Cookies to Bring)

A Christmas cookie exchange is one of the easiest holiday parties to host: everyone bakes one kind of cookie, and everyone goes home with a mixed box. It only goes wrong in three ways: five people bring chocolate chip, nobody knows how many to bake, or the rules arrive the night before. A short rules sheet and a sign-up list sent with the invitation fix all three. Below are rules you can copy, a swap math table, a few party ideas, and a free one-page printable with all of it.

Cookie exchange rules you can copy

  1. One kind of cookie per baker. Ask guests to claim their cookie on the sign-up sheet when they RSVP. First to claim keeps it.
  2. Set a number, not a vibe. "Bring 4 dozen" works; "bring plenty" doesn't. Use the table below.
  3. Label every plate with the cookie's name and what it contains, so guests can choose for themselves.
  4. Bring the recipe. A recipe card per baker (or a group chat photo) is half the fun of a swap.
  5. Bring a container to carry your mix home. Hosts can keep a few spare boxes for anyone who forgets.
  6. Taste first, then swap. A tasting plate and a quick vote gives the party a middle, not just a pick-up.

Store-bought or home-baked? Decide before you send the invitation and say so in the rules. Many groups allow a "favourite shortcut" (slice-and-bake, a mix with your own twist) so busy guests still come.

How many cookies should each person bring?

The formula is simple: number of bakers × cookies each guest takes home + a few for the tasting plate. Each baker brings that many. With 6 extra for tasting:

6 bakers30 cookies (2.5 dozen)42 cookies (3.5 dozen)
8 bakers38 cookies (3.5 dozen)54 cookies (4.5 dozen)
10 bakers46 cookies (4 dozen)66 cookies (5.5 dozen)
12 bakers54 cookies (4.5 dozen)78 cookies (6.5 dozen)

Dozens are rounded up to the nearest half dozen. Past about 10 bakers, "4 of each" keeps the baking reasonable; with 6 or fewer, 6 of each still gives everyone a full box. If someone cancels, the swap still works: everyone just takes home a little more of each.

Sign-up sheet: what to ask for

Keep the sign-up short: name, cookie, how many dozen, and tick boxes for what it contains (nuts, dairy, egg, wheat, plus an "other" note). Share it as a printed sheet at work, or send a photo of it in the group chat and update it as people claim cookies. The tick boxes are a courtesy, not a guarantee, so anyone avoiding an ingredient should still check with the baker.

Cookie exchange party ideas that take no extra work

  • Cookie awards. Paper ballots for "best decorated", "most festive" and "I need this recipe", with a small certificate for each winner.
  • A plate-label table. Fold-over tent cards in front of every plate double as decoration.
  • Take-home tags. Tie a tag to each box with the baker list, so people remember whose cookie was whose.
  • A recipe swap binder. Collect the recipe cards and send everyone a photo afterwards.
  • Timing. Send invitations three weeks out, the rules and sign-up two weeks out, and a reminder with the final count three days before.

Hosting at the office? The same rules work; just pick a room, a time slot and a cut-off for the sign-up sheet.
