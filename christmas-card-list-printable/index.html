Christmas Card List Printable (Plus 2026 Mail-By Dates)

Every year the same three questions come up: who's on the list, where do they live now, and did they send us one last year? A simple Christmas card list answers all three, and it saves you from rebuilding it from old envelopes each December. How to build your card list in 20 minutes

  1. Start from last year. Pull names from last year's list, the cards you received, and your phone contacts. One line per household, not per person.
  2. Sort into groups: family, friends, neighbours, work, and people abroad. People abroad need their cards mailed first.
  3. Check every address before you write. Text anyone who moved this year. Put a tick in the "A" box once an address is confirmed, so you never address an envelope from memory.
  4. Count and budget. Total households = cards needed. Add three or four spares for the cards that arrive from people you forgot. Then count stamps for local and international mail separately, since international postage costs more.
  5. Tick "S" as each card goes in the mailbox and "R" when one arrives. In January that column tells you who to add, and who you can drop with a clear conscience.

What to write next to each name

  • The household name as you'd write it on the envelope ("The Nguyen family", "Sam & Priya")
  • Full mailing address, including apartment or unit number and postal or ZIP code
  • Kids' and pets' names, if you include them in the message
  • Card type: photo card, boxed card or a longer note
  • Anything worth remembering: new baby, new house, a loss this year

A calm card timeline

  • Early November: update addresses, choose photo cards or boxed cards, count how many you need.
  • Mid November: take the family photo, buy stamps, print return-address labels.
  • Late November: write and sign in batches of ten, and mail international cards first.
  • Early December: mail local cards, log the cards that arrive and add new addresses as they come in.

Keep it for next year

A one-year list works, but a multi-year log is where it pays off: you can see at a glance who you've swapped cards with since 2026, and the address book only needs updating, not rewriting. Keep the pages with your Christmas planner so they're easy to find next November.
