What goes on a holiday shopping list

A useful holiday shopping list has six columns: who the gift is for, the gift idea, your budget for that person, where you'll buy it, what you actually paid, and two boxes to tick when it's bought and wrapped. The paid column matters most. It's what tells you, in the middle of Cyber Week, how much of your total is really left.

Page 2 of the free printable is a deal plan for Black Friday and Cyber Monday: the item, its regular price, the top price you'll pay, the store and the sale day. There's also a six-question check to run before you buy, plus space for food and hosting and for stocking stuffers, which are where holiday budgets usually leak.

How to use it during Black Friday and Cyber Week

  1. List the people first. Write every name on page 1 before you open a single sale email. Include teachers, hosts, neighbours and the gift exchange at work.
  2. Set one total, then split it. Pick a total you can pay in cash this month and keep 10% back for the gifts you forget. With CA$600 and 8 people, that's CA$60 held back and about CA$67 each.
  3. Write your top price before the sale. On page 2, note the regular price and the most you'll pay. On sale day you only buy what lands at or under that number.
  4. Shop the list on the big days. Black Friday is Friday, November 27, Cyber Monday is Monday, November 30 and, in Canada, Boxing Day is Saturday, December 26. Most online deals run the whole week in between.
  5. Tick bought and wrapped. Mark B and W as you go, keep receipts in one envelope and note return windows for anything opened after Christmas.

Why a printed list helps on sale days

Sale pages are designed to make you add things. A list on paper, with a price you decided calmly a week earlier, turns a deal into a yes-or-no question: is it on the list, and is it under my number? If both answers are yes, buy it and tick the box. If not, close the tab. Digital downloads are also a handy last-minute gift because they arrive by email straight away, with no shipping deadline.
