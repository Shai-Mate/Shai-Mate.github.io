How the 52 week savings challenge works

Each week you put away the week's number in dollars: $1 in week 1, $2 in week 2, all the way to $52 in week 52. The first weeks are easy, which is the point: you build the habit before the amounts get big. Add every week together and you have $1,378 by the end of the year without ever finding a large lump sum.

Five steps to finish the year

  1. Pick a version you can finish. The classic plan asks for $202 in December. If that month is already tight, choose the reverse or the flat plan instead.
  2. Choose your deposit day. 2027 starts on a Friday, so a Friday plan gives clean weeks: week 1 is January 1 and week 52 is December 24.
  3. Give the money its own place. A separate savings account or a labelled envelope stops challenge money from leaking back into groceries.
  4. Tick it the same day. Write the amount, tick the box and update the running total as soon as the transfer is done.
  5. Plan for a missed week. Decide now: catch up the next week, or swap that week's amount with a smaller one later. Either keeps the total.

Where the money goes

A challenge works best with a named goal: a holiday fund, a car repair cushion or next year's gifts. If you have several goals, split them into sinking funds and give each one its own line, and plan the rest of the year in a 2027 budget planner.

Questions people ask

How much do you save with the 52 week savings challenge?

$1,378 with the classic version ($1 in week 1 rising by $1 to $52 in week 52). Doubling every amount gives $2,756; $5 steps give $6,890.

Is the reverse 52 week challenge better?

It saves the same $1,378 but puts the big amounts at the start of the year, while the habit is fresh, and only $10 in December instead of $202.

When should I start the 52 week challenge in 2027?

Any week works, but a Friday start lines up with the calendar: Friday, January 1 to Friday, December 24, one week before New Year's Eve.

What is the 100 envelope challenge?

You number 100 envelopes or squares, then put in the amount on each one in any order. Using every number from 1 to 100 adds up to $5,050, so most people spread it over more than a year or halve the amounts.
