Stocking Stuffer Ideas Printable: Budget Per Person, Ideas by Age and a Free Budget Sheet

Stockings are the part of Christmas that gets bought last and in a hurry, usually one impulse purchase at a time. A few minutes of planning makes them cheaper and more thoughtful: decide what each stocking costs, split that number across stuffers, and write ideas down per person before you shop. Below is a budget split table, ideas by age, and a free one-page sheet to print.

Step 1: set one budget per stocking

Pick a number first, per person, and keep it the same for people in the same group (all the kids, all the adults). Equal budgets avoid awkward comparisons, and a fixed number stops the small purchases from creeping up. Add the stocking totals to your main gift budget so they don't surprise you in January.

Step 2: split it across stuffers

The same budget buys very different stockings depending on how many items you want to unwrap. This table is simple division, so you can see what each stuffer can cost:

For example, a $20 stocking built from five small stuffers leaves $4.00 for each. Mix them: one $8 treat, a few $2 fillers, and something practical you would have bought anyway, like socks or lip balm.

Step 3: choose from a balanced list

A stocking feels full when it has a mix: something to wear, read, eat, use and do. These generic ideas are grouped by age:

Paper experiences cost almost nothing and are often the favourite: a coupon for a movie night or a promise of a pancake breakfast takes a minute to write.

Step 4: write it down and tick it off

Keep one card per stocking with the name, the budget, each idea with its cost, and a tick when bought. Then total what you have spent against the budget. Do the same for bigger gifts so the stocking spending is not hidden. If you shop across stores and online, a simple tracker with item, store, price and ordered, arrived and wrapped ticks avoids duplicates and missing orders.
