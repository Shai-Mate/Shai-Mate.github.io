24 Kids Advent Calendar Ideas

An activity advent calendar swaps a daily treat for a small thing you do together. Most of the ideas below cost little or nothing, take 10 to 45 minutes, and work for a wide range of ages. Pick the ones that fit your family, move them around to match your calendar, and keep it fun rather than another chore.

The 24 ideas

  1. Make paper snowflakes and tape them to a window.
  2. Write or draw a letter to a grandparent or friend who lives far away.
  3. Bake and decorate simple sugar cookies (a grown-up handles the oven).
  4. Build a blanket fort and read winter stories by flashlight.
  5. Make a paper chain garland for a doorway.
  6. Go on an evening walk to look at the neighbourhood lights.
  7. Pick one toy or book in good condition to donate.
  8. Make pinecone bird feeders with a seed spread and hang them outside.
  9. Have a family movie night with homemade popcorn.
  10. Draw homemade holiday cards for neighbours or classmates.
  11. Hold a kitchen dance party to holiday music.
  12. Kindness day: help a neighbour, carry groceries, or hold the door for people.
  13. Make salt-dough ornaments and paint them once they dry.
  14. Host a hot cocoa taste test with different toppings.
  15. Do an indoor scavenger hunt for five red, five green and five sparkly things.
  16. Wrap a small gift for someone in the family, all by yourself.
  17. Make thank-you cards for a teacher, bus driver or mail carrier.
  18. Play a board game or card game night.
  19. Eat breakfast for dinner in pyjamas.
  20. Build a snow creature, or a cardboard one if there's no snow.
  21. Write a family time capsule note: favourite moments of the year.
  22. Sing carols for a neighbour or on a video call with relatives.
  23. Have a winter picnic on the living room floor.
  24. Lay out pyjamas, read a favourite story, and write wishes for the new year.

How to set it up

  • Plan around your real December first. Look at concerts, work parties and travel days, and put the quick ideas (window snowflakes, a dance party) on the busy dates.
  • Hide each idea in a numbered envelope, a small box, a jar of folded slips, or on a printed countdown chart the kids colour in each day.
  • Keep a "swap list" of three 10-minute ideas for days when the plan falls apart. Nobody needs to know.
  • Prep supplies in one bin: paper, tape, glue, string, markers, a hole punch and cocoa. Most of the list is covered.

Adapting for different ages

  • Toddlers: keep it short and sensory: tearing paper for snowflakes, stirring dough, spotting lights from the stroller.
  • School-age kids: let them read the day's card and take the lead, and give them one "choose your own" day a week.
  • Tweens: hand over the kindness days and the time capsule. They often enjoy planning more than doing.

Keep it about time together

The point is a small, shared moment each day. If you miss a day, double up or skip it. A countdown chart on the fridge makes the progress visible, and kids love colouring in each square.
