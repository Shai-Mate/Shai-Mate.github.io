Paper: what works best

  • Card stock in the 65 to 110 lb range (about 175 to 300 gsm) feels sturdy and won't tear at the hole. Check what your printer can feed.
  • Matte is easier to write on than glossy, and ballpoint and gel pens won't smudge.
  • Kraft or cream card gives a softer, vintage look. Bright white makes colours pop.
  • No card stock? Print on plain paper and glue it onto a cereal-box panel before cutting.

Printing tips

  1. Print at 100% or "actual size", not "fit to page", so the tags stay the right size.
  2. Use your printer's thick-paper or card setting and feed one sheet at a time if it jams.
  3. Let the ink dry for a few minutes before cutting.

Cutting and attaching

  • A paper trimmer or a metal ruler and craft knife gives straighter edges than scissors.
  • Round the corners with scissors or a corner punch for a finished look.
  • Punch the marked circle with a single-hole punch and loop through ribbon, baker's twine or leftover yarn.
  • No hole punch? Tape the tag flat to the gift, or tuck it under the ribbon.

Little tricks

  • Colour-code by person so kids can spot their gifts at a glance (and you can spot a missing one).
  • Number secret gifts and keep the key on your gift tracker, so nobody peeks at a name.
  • Write tags as you wrap. Unlabelled boxes are how the wrong gift ends up with the wrong cousin.
  • Short notes win: "Open me first," "For the cocoa shelf," or a one-line inside joke.
