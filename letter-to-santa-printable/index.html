Letter to Santa Printable (Where to Mail It in 2026 + How to Write the Reply)

Writing to Santa is one of the few Christmas traditions that costs almost nothing and gives you a keepsake every year. The hard parts are practical: where the letter goes, how early to mail it, what a five-year-old can actually write, and what to do if no reply comes back. This guide covers all four, with a free planner and keepsake log to print.

In both countries, write your return address clearly on the envelope and inside the letter. Canada Post also asks families to put all their children's letters in one envelope. Dates and addresses were checked on October 26, 2026 on each postal service's own site; check again before you mail, and mail a week or two early if you can.

What kids can write at each age

  • Ages 3 to 5: a drawing does most of the work. Let them dictate two or three sentences, then they sign their name (or a big letter of it).
  • Ages 6 to 8: fill-in prompts work best: "This year I learned...", "I was proud when...", "My top wish is...", "A question for Santa:".
  • Ages 9 and up: a free letter on lined paper. Suggest one question for Santa and one thing they want to do for someone else. It keeps the letter from becoming only a list.

Keep the wish list on its own page. It is easier to share with grandparents, and the letter itself stays about the child.

How to write a reply from the North Pole yourself

If the mail doesn't come back in time, or you would rather answer at home, a short reply works best when it is specific:

  1. Quote one detail from their letter ("I loved the drawing of your dog in the snow").
  2. Name one real thing they did this year, like learning to swim or helping a sibling.
  3. Keep the wishes open: "The elves and I will see what we can do" protects you if the top wish isn't under the tree.
  4. Change the handwriting: use print capitals, a different pen colour, or type the letter and sign it by hand.
  5. Time it: leave it with the stockings on Christmas Eve, or put it in the mailbox about ten days after the letter went out.

Make it a yearly tradition

Photograph or copy every letter before it is mailed and record it in the keepsake log: year, age, top wish and the kind thing they promised. In ten years the log is the part everyone wants to read.
