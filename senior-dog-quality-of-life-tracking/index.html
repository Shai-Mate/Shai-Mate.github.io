How to Track a Senior Dog's Quality of Life

Older dogs change slowly, which makes it hard to notice trends. A few notes a day turn "I think he's slowing down" into something clear you can talk through with your vet.

Five things worth noting daily

  1. Appetite and water: normal, less or none
  2. Mobility: stairs, getting up, walk length
  3. Comfort: panting at rest, restlessness at night, signs of pain
  4. Toileting: accidents, changes in stool or urine
  5. Joy: greeting you, interest in toys, treats and walks

Good days vs bad days

Many owners mark each day as good or bad on a calendar. When bad days start to outnumber good ones, it's a sign to book a conversation with your vet. Published quality-of-life scales, such as the HHHHHMM scale vets use, can guide that talk.

Keep medications in the same place

Senior dogs often take several medicines and supplements. Track them next to the symptoms so you can see what changed when. Try the free pet medication log.
