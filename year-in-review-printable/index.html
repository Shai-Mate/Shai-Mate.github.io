What goes in a year in review

A good year in review is short enough to finish in one sitting. It covers five things: the moments you want to remember, what you're proud of, what you learned, what didn't work, and the people you're grateful for. Then you score each area of life from 1 to 5: health and energy, work, home, fun, money, relationships, learning and giving. The scores show, in one glance, where 2027 needs attention.

12 year-end reflection questions

Memories

  1. What were the three best days of 2026, and what made them good?
  2. Which photo from this year would you frame?
  3. What did you try for the first time?

Wins

  1. What are you proud of, even if nobody else noticed?
  2. What got easier for you this year?
  3. Which small habit actually stuck?

Lessons

  1. What did not work, and what would you do differently?
  2. What did you spend time or money on that wasn't worth it?
  3. What is one thing you want to leave in 2026?

People

  1. Who made your year better, and have you told them?
  2. Who do you want to see more of in 2027?
  3. What did someone teach you this year?

A year in review for kids

Children enjoy looking back as much as adults, as long as it feels like drawing, not homework. Use sentence starters and big boxes:

  • My favourite day this year was ...
  • Something new I learned to do
  • My best friend this year
  • A food I tried for the first time
  • Something that made me laugh
  • Next year I want to learn ...

Ages 4 to 7 can draw and tell you the words; ages 8 to 12 can write their own and add a goal for next year. Date and keep each sheet. It makes a calm activity for the afternoon of a family New Year's Eve.

How to do it in five steps

  1. Pick a quiet hour. Christmas Day falls on a Friday this year and New Year's Day on a Friday, so the days in between (December 27-30) are usually the calmest time for it.
  2. Look back before you write. Scroll your phone photos and calendar month by month. You'll remember far more than you expect.
  3. Answer the questions. Write short answers. A few honest words beat a polished paragraph.
  4. Rate each life area 1 to 5. The lowest one or two scores are where a 2027 goal will make the biggest difference.
  5. Turn one lesson into one goal. Take one thing that didn't work and write the opposite as a goal for 2027.

From review to 2027 goals

Your lowest-rated area and your "didn't work" answer are where to start. If money scored a 2, a 2027 budget planner or a no-spend January is a concrete first step. Keep it to one or two goals per area, break each into quarterly steps, and check in once a month. A 2027 printable planner gives you the dated pages to track it.

Questions people ask

What should I write in a year in review?

Your best moments, what you are proud of, what you learned, what didn't work, who you are grateful for and a 1-to-5 rating for each area of life. Then pick one or two things to change next year.

How do I do a year in review with kids?

Keep it short and visual: a drawing box for their favourite day, a few sentence starters like "Something new I learned to do" and one wish for next year. Young children can tell you their answers while you write.

When is the best time to do a year in review?

Any time in the last week of December or the first week of January. In 2026, the days between Christmas and Thursday, December 31 are a natural slot, before 2027 plans start.

Is a year in review the same as New Year's resolutions?

No. The review looks back at 2026; resolutions or goals look forward to 2027. Doing the review first makes your goals more realistic, because they come from what actually happened.
