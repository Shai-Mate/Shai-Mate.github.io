Christmas Dinner Planner Printable: 2026 Countdown, Menu and Turkey Size

Christmas Day 2026 falls on a Friday, so most hosts have Thursday, Christmas Eve, for the last round of prep. Dinner goes smoothly when three things are written down early: who is coming (and what they can't eat), the menu and who makes each dish, and when each job happens. Here is a dated countdown, a simple way to build the menu, a turkey size and thawing table, and a free one-page countdown sheet to print.

Build the menu by course

Plan in courses rather than as one long list. It shows where you have too much (three potato dishes) or too little (nothing for a vegetarian guest). Pick sides that use different cooking methods so they don't all need the oven.

CourseHow many dishesEasy examples
Starters & nibbles2 to 3Cheese board, dips, a soup that reheats
Main dish1 (plus a vegetarian main if needed)Roast turkey, ham or a nut roast
Sides4 to 6Potatoes two ways, a green vegetable, a roasted root vegetable, stuffing
Sauces & extras2 to 4Gravy, cranberry sauce, bread rolls, butter
Desserts2One made ahead, one bought or brought by a guest
Drinks2 to 3 plus waterOne festive drink, one alcohol-free option

Write a name beside every dish. Give helpful guests a course and a size ("a dessert for ten"). Keep an allergen line under the menu and label dishes on the day.

How big a turkey do you need?

The USDA's planning figure is about 1 lb of whole turkey per person, or 1.5 lb if you want leftovers. For a boneless roast, plan less.

When to start thawing a frozen turkey

In the fridge, allow roughly one day for every 4 to 5 lb. Counting back from Christmas morning:

Turkey weightFridge thawingStart by
8 to 12 lb (3.6 to 5.4 kg)3 daysTue Dec 22
12 to 16 lb (5.4 to 7.3 kg)4 daysMon Dec 21
16 to 20 lb (7.3 to 9.1 kg)5 daysSun Dec 20
20 to 24 lb (9.1 to 10.9 kg)6 daysSat Dec 19

These are planning dates only: always follow the thawing, cooking and temperature guidance on your turkey's label and in your recipe.

Split the work: make-ahead vs. on the day

  • Before Christmas Eve: sauces, desserts, chopped vegetables.
  • Christmas Eve: casseroles and stuffing assembled, table set.
  • On the day: only the roast, potatoes, greens and gravy; everything else is reheating.
