Christmas Party Games for Work (Printable, Office-Friendly)

The best office Christmas party games are short, need no setup and work for people who barely know each other. Nobody should have to perform, drink or share anything personal to join in. Below are six games that fit a meeting room and a lunch hour, with group sizes, timings and how to run each one, plus a 60-minute plan and a free one-page game plan you can print.

How to run them without awkward moments

  • Bingo first. It needs no teams and no talking, so it warms up a room of people from different departments. Give each person a different card and have the caller cross words off a master sheet so a winner can be checked in seconds.
  • Mix the trivia teams. Number people off 1-4 instead of letting departments sit together. Keep questions to general holiday knowledge (songs, foods, winter facts), not inside jokes about colleagues.
  • Set the gift swap rules in writing. Send the spending limit, the "steal" limit and the start time with the invitation. A limit of two steals per gift stops one present from bouncing around for ten minutes.
  • Keep the scavenger hunt to shared spaces. Things like "something red on a desk" or "a mug with writing on it" are fine; nobody's drawers or private offices.
  • Make sitting out easy. Scorekeeper, timekeeper and caller are real jobs. Offer them to anyone who would rather not play.

Inclusive and no-alcohol options

Every game above works with coffee and cookies at 2 PM. Use "holiday" or "winter" wording where you can, so colleagues who don't celebrate Christmas still feel the games are for them, and avoid games that involve physical contact, eating challenges or guessing anyone's age, weight or salary. For remote and hybrid teams, bingo, trivia and would-you-rather all work over a video call: share the cards as a PDF and have people hold up a hand or type in the chat.

A 60-minute party plan

  1. 0:00-0:10 Arrive, drinks and snacks, hand out bingo cards.
  2. 0:10-0:30 Christmas bingo, three rounds (one line, two lines, full card).
  3. 0:30-0:50 Team trivia, or the gift swap if you are doing one.
  4. 0:50-1:00 Would you rather to wind down, prizes, thanks.

Got two hours? Add the scavenger hunt after trivia and run the gift swap last, so people leave with their present.

Quick planning checklist

  • Three weeks out: book the room, pick the date, set a prize budget and the gift swap limit.
  • Two weeks out: send the invitation with the gift swap rules and a link for remote staff.
  • Three days out: print cards and sheets, count pens, buy small prizes (gift cards, treats, a "best team" trophy).
  • On the day: one person calls, one keeps score, one runs the clock.

The same games work for family gatherings, church halls and table games at a community dinner: just swap the office scavenger hunt for a house or hall version.
