What to put in an emergency binder: the 8 sections

The binder answers every question someone would otherwise phone you about. Eight sections cover most households:

SectionWhat to record
Emergency numbersEmergency and non-emergency lines, utility outage lines, your address as you'd read it to a call-taker
PeopleEveryone in the house, family and friends to call, the neighbour with a key, your plumber and electrician
HomeSpare keys, where the water main, electrical panel and gas shut-off are (sketch them), utility accounts, warranties
VehiclesMake, model and plate, insurer and where the policy is, roadside assistance number, service log
Papers and accountsWhere policies, IDs and documents are kept, which banks you use, digital accounts and subscriptions
BillsMonthly due dates, plus yearly renewals like insurance, licences and memberships
Sitter pagesHouse-sitter instructions and daily routines, a babysitter page, a pet care page
The planWhere to meet, an out-of-area contact, a go-bag checklist, the date you last reviewed it

Short on time? Do the numbers and the shut-offs first.

What to leave out

Anyone can open a binder on a shelf, so it should say where things are and who to call, never the secrets themselves. Keep these out:

  • Passwords and PINs
  • Alarm codes
  • Full account or card numbers
  • Social Insurance or Social Security numbers

Write "ask Sam" or "grey file box, hall closet" instead. The binder points the way; it doesn't hold the keys.

How to make a household binder in an afternoon

  1. Walk the house. Find the water main, electrical panel and gas shut-off; note or sketch where each is.
  2. Empty your phone into it. Copy emergency numbers, family, neighbours and trades into the contact pages.
  3. Note where papers live. Don't move documents into the binder; write where each one is kept.
  4. Add the bills. List each bill and its due day from one month of statements.
  5. Write the sitter pages. A normal day for the house, kids or pet, and who to call if something goes wrong.
  6. Shelve it. Use a 1-inch binder with tabs, tell everyone where it lives, and write the next review date on the front.

For the bills pages, the bill tracker guide shows how to match each bill to a payday; yearly costs belong on a sinking funds list.

When to review it: 2027 dates

Use the clock-change Sundays: in 2027, Sun Mar 14 and Sun Nov 7 in most of Canada and the US. Update sooner after a move, a new car or a new pet.

Away over the holidays? Christmas Day 2026 is a Friday. If someone is watching the house or the pet, fill in the sitter pages by Sat Dec 19 so there's time to walk them through.

Where to keep it

Somewhere obvious, spine labelled. For the emergency plan and kit itself, follow official guidance: Get Prepared (Government of Canada) or Ready.gov in the US.

Questions people ask

Should I put passwords in an emergency binder?

No. Anyone in the house can open it. Write where that information is kept or who to ask instead.

How often should I update an emergency binder?

Twice a year, plus after a move, a new car or a new pet.

Is an emergency binder the same as a household binder?

They overlap. A household binder runs the house week to week; the emergency section is what someone else would need if you weren't there. Many families keep one binder with both.
