Christmas Eve Box Ideas (by Age) + Printable Labels

A Christmas Eve box is a small box of cosy things opened on the night before Christmas: usually new pyjamas, something to read, a warm drink and an activity for the evening. It gives excited kids something to open and makes bedtime easier. Below are ideas by age with low-cost items, a packing timeline, budget tips and a free one-page planner you can print.

The classic formula is something to wear, something to read, something to sip and something to do. Pick one from each and you have a full box without overspending.

Budget tips

  • Set a number per box first. CA$15-25 per child is plenty. Write it on the planner before you shop.
  • Move, don't add. Pyjamas, a book or a family game you were already buying for Christmas can go in the box instead of under the tree.
  • Use the library and the pantry. A borrowed holiday film, home-made popcorn and cocoa from the cupboard cost almost nothing.
  • Reuse the box. A shoebox or basket works every year; only the label changes.
  • Print the extras. A colouring page, a scavenger hunt or a letter costs a sheet of paper and fills the evening.

How to pack it and when

  1. Early December: decide who gets a box and set a budget per box
  2. Mid December: buy or gather items; keep a list so nothing is doubled up
  3. December 20-22: print and fill in the labels, letter and tags
  4. December 23: pack each box, tuck the letter on top, close and label
  5. Christmas Eve: hand the boxes out after dinner or leave them by each bed

Put the letter or note on top so it is the first thing they see, then soft items, then the book and the snack. Label each box clearly so siblings grab the right one.

Starting a family tradition

The boxes people remember are the ones that repeat. A few ideas that grow with the family:

  • A letter every year. Write a short note about something each child did that year. Keep a copy and you end up with a record of every Christmas.
  • One shared activity. The same evening game or film each year, so the box becomes the start of the night rather than a second Christmas.
  • Older kids help. Once children outgrow their own box, let them pack one for a younger sibling or cousin.
  • Not only for kids. A box for a grandparent, a friend spending the holidays alone or houseguests is a simple, kind gesture.
