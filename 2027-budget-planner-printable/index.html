2027 budget planner: what to include, your 2027 paydays, and a free printable

Short answer

A good 2027 budget planner needs a year-at-a-glance page, a monthly budget for each month with planned and actual columns, a bill tracker, a sinking funds page for yearly costs, a debt payoff page and a savings tracker. Before you fill in January, check your 2027 pay schedule: 2027 starts and ends on a Friday, so anyone paid every Friday gets 53 paycheques, and biweekly Friday paydays that start January 1 give 27 paycheques, with three-paycheque months in January, July and December.

What a 2027 budget planner should include

You can do a lot with one page a month. But a planner that holds up for a whole year has six parts, and each one does a different job:

PageWhat it's for
Year at a glanceIncome, spending, savings and debt paid for all 12 months on one page. This is where you see the year's trend.
Monthly budget (x12)Plan vs actual for each month: income, fixed bills, day-to-day spending, savings and debt. See how to make a monthly budget.
Bill trackerEvery bill, its due day and a paid box for each month. See how to keep track of bills.
Sinking fundsMoney set aside monthly for yearly costs like insurance and Christmas. See sinking fund categories.
Debt payoffEach debt, its payoff order and the balance month by month. See snowball vs avalanche.
Savings goalsTwo to four goals with a target and progress you can see.

An expense log is useful too, especially for the first three months, while you learn where the money actually goes.

How many paycheques do you get in 2027?

January 1, 2027 is a Friday, and so is December 31. That gives 2027 53 Fridays, and it changes the math for anyone paid on Fridays:

Pay schedulePaycheques in 2027Months that are different
Weekly, every Friday53January, April, July, October and December have 5 Fridays
Every second Friday, first payday Jan 1273 paydays in January, July and December
Every second Friday, first payday Jan 8263 paydays in April and October
15th and last day of the month24Always 2 a month
Monthly12Always 1 a month

Two things to do with this:

  • Budget each month on the normal number (two cheques if biweekly, four if weekly), and decide now where the extra cheques go. Write it on the year-at-a-glance page: "July 30 cheque: car repair fund."
  • If you're salaried and paid biweekly from January 1, check your first January pay stub. In a 27-pay year, some employers split the annual salary into 27 smaller cheques instead of 26. If yours does, each cheque is about 3.7% smaller, and your monthly budget needs to start from the smaller number.

Set up your 2027 budget in one hour

  1. Write your 2027 income (10 minutes). Look up your pay schedule above, count your paycheques per month and write them on the year-at-a-glance page. Mark the extra-cheque months.
  2. List the year's irregular costs (15 minutes). Go through 2026's statements for anything that wasn't monthly. Add the dates from the table above. Total each category.
  3. Turn them into sinking funds (5 minutes). Divide each yearly total by 12 (or by the months left before it's due). The sum is one line in every monthly budget.
  4. Fill in your bill tracker (10 minutes). Every bill, sorted by due day.
  5. Write January's budget (15 minutes). Income, bills, day-to-day spending from real statement numbers, debt, savings, sinking funds. Make it add up to zero.
  6. Book your reviews (5 minutes). Put a 20-minute review in your calendar for the last day of each month, and a longer one around June 30, halfway through the year.

If you start in December, January's budget is ready before the January credit card statement shows up with Christmas on it. That's also a good month to pair the budget with a no spend January.

Dated or undated?

A dated 2027 planner has the months and days already printed, so there's nothing to set up and you can see paydays and weekends at a glance. An undated one can start any month and you can reuse it, but you write in every date yourself. If you're starting in January and plan to do the whole year, dated is less work. If you've started and stopped budgets before, undated is more forgiving.

Paper, PDF or spreadsheet?

  • Printed pages in a binder: best if you like seeing the whole month at once and writing it by hand. Print the year at a glance and bill tracker first; print each month when it starts.
  • Fillable PDF: the same pages, typed on a computer or tablet. Good if you want it neat, or want to print a clean copy later.
  • Spreadsheet: best if you want totals and charts done for you. Less pleasant to look at, much faster to update.

Printing tip: print at "Actual size" or 100%, not "Fit to page", so boxes and lines come out the size they were designed. US Letter is standard in Canada and the US; A4 everywhere else.

What to do in the first week of January

Don't try to perfect the whole year. In week one, only three things matter: January's budget is written, the bill tracker is filled in, and you've moved the first sinking-fund amount into savings. Everything else can wait for the January 31 review.

Questions people ask

When should I start my 2027 budget?

In December 2026, if you can. Writing January's budget before January 1 means it's ready before the holiday credit card bills arrive. Starting later in the year works too; begin with the current month and fill in the year-at-a-glance page from there.

How many paycheques are there in 2027?

It depends on your schedule. 2027 has 53 Fridays, so weekly Friday pay gives 53 paycheques. Every-second-Friday pay starting January 1 gives 27 (three in January, July and December); starting January 8 gives 26 (three in April and October). Semi-monthly pay gives 24.

Should I get a dated or undated budget planner?

Dated is less work if you're starting in January and doing the whole year. Undated is more flexible if you might start mid-year or skip months.

Should I print my budget planner on Letter or A4?

Use US Letter in Canada and the United States, A4 in most other countries. Print at Actual size or 100% so the boxes print the size they were designed.

Is a spreadsheet better than a printable budget planner?

Neither is better; it depends on what you'll use every week. Spreadsheets do the math, printables are easier to see at a glance. Many people use both: a spreadsheet for tracking and a printed plan on the fridge.
